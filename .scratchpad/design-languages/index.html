What LlamaTrade is

LlamaTrade is a multi-tenant platform for rules-based investing on your own Alpaca brokerage account. You write a strategy as target weights across US stocks and ETFs, backtest it on daily history, and deploy it to trade on a schedule. It's aimed at self-directed traders who want tactical allocation or momentum rotation without building the infrastructure. It's in a closed, invite-only paper-trading beta; real-money trading is a separately gated milestone.

1

Build

Blocks, code or the copilot all edit one S-expression language. It returns weights, never share counts: 17 indicators, six weighting methods, conditions across symbols.

2

Version

Saving parses and validates the text and writes an immutable version. Anything the engine can't run, such as market-cap weighting, is refused at this point.

3

Backtest

A Celery worker replays daily bars through the same StrategySession used live, fills at the close with slippage, and reports Sharpe, Sortino, drawdown and a benchmark.

4

Deploy

Starting a session funds a sleeve in the ledger, then the trading service streams bars, opens the rebalance gate on schedule and runs seven risk checks per order.

5

Account

Fills post to a double-entry ledger with lots per sleeve. Reconciliation against Alpaca freezes a sleeve on any quantity mismatch rather than correcting it.

How it works underneath

  • Nine Python services (auth, strategy, backtest, market-data, trading, portfolio, notification, billing, agent) and a React SPA, talking Connect RPC with protobuf types. No gateway.
  • Data: Alpaca's IEX feed, about 3% of consolidated volume, stored in TimescaleDB. Backtest datasets are content-addressed Parquet snapshots.
  • Engine: one evaluation core for backtest and live. Live folds minute bars into a daily bar and evaluates only when every symbol has data. Missing values count as degraded evaluations.
  • Risk: market hours, order value, sleeve gate, allowed symbols, position size, daily loss and order rate, plus a broker circuit breaker. Failing to read the sleeve rejects the order.
  • Money: an event-sourced double-entry ledger is the book of record. Sleeves let several strategies share one Alpaca account without touching each other's holdings.
  • Scope: long-only US equities and ETFs, daily or slower rebalancing, margin accounts. Shared Postgres with row-level security per tenant.

The current design: Monolith

Neo-brutalist on warm paper: bone #FBF8F1, ink #0D0D0D and signal orange #FF4D1C; Anton for condensed uppercase display, Archivo for body and Space Mono for labels and figures; zero radius, hard 4 to 12 px ink shadows, and a 12-column grid of vertical lines behind everything. Tokens live in monolith.css and drive Tailwind through CSS variables. Dark mode was retired in code; the dark capture below is an injected preview.

What works

  • It is memorable and consistent across web and mobile.
  • The token layer is clean: one CSS file reskins the app, and React Native mirrors it.
  • Figures are monospaced and aligned; the benchmark is a dashed line.
  • The DSL is shown openly, which suits the product.
  • Several empty states are already honest.

What's generic

  • Brutalist poster style is a broad indie-software trend, not something drawn from trading.
  • Orange is the brand, primary buttons, Paper tags, alerts, badges and the copilot, so it carries no single meaning, and it sits close to loss red.
  • Every tile has a border and a hard shadow, so dense data gets noisy and nothing leads.
  • Uppercase mono labels everywhere slow reading.

What's missing for trading

  • No designed system for states: frozen sleeve, waiting for data, degraded evaluation, risk rejection.
  • Live and paper differ only by an orange tag.
  • No dark mode for long market-hours sessions.
  • No figure conventions: signs, decimals, negatives, timestamps with zones.
  • The strategy identity colours include green and red, which collide with P&L.

The README's preview.png shows an older, rounded design with a green save button and blue weight chips, so it no longer matches the app.

Three directions

Each takes its point of view from a different part of the product: the ledger, the runtime and the strategy language. They differ in type, colour, density, data display, motion and voice. All three keep green and red for P&L only, one accent for interaction, and amber for things that need attention.

C. Weights

The strategy language returns weights, so proportion becomes the main visual. One primitive, a bar that sums to 100%, appears for the account, each sleeve, each weight block and each month of a backtest. Target versus actual is a tick, so drift is visible at a glance.

Marketing sites

Each direction also has a full marketing site with the same twelve parts, facts and calls to action as the real one. All three lead with stacked app previews and a film strip of real screens, and have switchable margin art.

App screens: all 49 views of the app, desktop and phone, in the chosen design.

Flow variations, several layouts per flow with a recommended plan: auth, onboarding and home, library and builder, backtests and sessions, portfolio, copilot and account.

Type. The site, app screens and flows share one technical type system in both modes, switchable from the bar's Font menu: Geist with Geist Mono, IBM Plex Sans with Plex Mono, Inter Tight with JetBrains Mono, Space Grotesk with Space Mono, JetBrains Mono on its own, and Chivo with Chivo Mono. The pick is Geist with Geist Mono: it stays calm at hero size, its mono keeps prices and the backtest panel aligned and readable at small sizes, and it reads the same on light and black.

Logo directions: three families of marks, with Candlesticks chosen. Nav lockups: the chosen mark and wordmark at real size in the nav, with a larger mark and a quieter word.

Recommendation

Direction A, Statement

LlamaTrade's strongest and most specific idea is that money state is correct: a double-entry ledger, sleeves that can't touch each other, reconciliation that stops trading rather than guessing. A statement is the visual form people already associate with that, and its conventions (parentheses, subtotal and total rules, footnotes) carry meaning without explanation.

It also handles the product's honesty requirements well. Footnotes give the IEX coverage, the paper-only beta and the long-only scope a permanent place, and the navy accent never competes with P&L colours.

What to take from the others

From B, the status rail and the state shapes for session screens, since A alone doesn't say whether a session is waiting, degraded or frozen. These fit A's rules with A's fonts and colours.

From C, the target-versus-actual tick for sleeve holdings and the allocation preview in the editor. They show drift better than a column of percentages.

A is also the cheapest to build: it removes more than it adds, and most changes land in the token file and a few shared table and figure components.

All figures on these pages are illustrative and generated for the demos. The account, strategies and backtest are consistent across the three directions so they can be compared directly.
