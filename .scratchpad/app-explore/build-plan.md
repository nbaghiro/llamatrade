# Build plan: the options to build into apps/web

Generated from the round-one and round-two exploration pages. For each surface: the round-two recommendation, or round one's where no round-two surface replaced it. Overrides from the user are marked. Read the option's mockup in its file (search for the data-surface, then the data-opt) before building it.

Rules for reading this list:
- Where a round-one and a round-two surface cover the same thing (for example `home/dashboard` and `home/layout2`), the round-two one wins. Use the round-one surface only for what round two didn't cover.
- **Home follows `home-decisions.md`**, which the user agreed and which wins over anything below: the ordinary day is `home/layout2` A (the annotated curve), with live and paper as two small figures beside the total; when something needs you, an amber line under the greeting plus the affected lane turning amber (not `home/attention2` B); first visit is `home/first-visit2` B; first session is `home/first-session` B until about five trading days of history; more than eight sessions fold into the five largest plus an "N more" row; after the close uses `home/quiet` A in layout2's layout; phone is `home/phone` B. The `home/hero`, `home/sessions-block` and `home/activity` recommendations apply only where they fit inside layout2 A.
- **Backtest results (user override):** the user loves the marketing site's backtest preview (`apps/web/src/marketing/screens/BacktestScreen.tsx`: run log on the left, six figures with SPY under each, curve against SPY with the drawdown band below, then monthly returns and indicators and metrics side by side). Keep that screen as the base of the real results page (`pages/backtests/BacktestRunPage.tsx` and `components/backtest/*`, which already follow it), matching the preview closely. From `backtests/results2` A and `backtests/running2` A, add only the annotations (rebalance and stress markers on the curve, the curve drawing in while it runs) on top of it; don't replace the layout.
- Shell: the frame is `shell/frame2` A (seven areas: Markets becomes an area), and its other recommendations follow. Home's "Markets page" is built as that seventh area (`portfolio/markets2` A is its design).
- Where a design needs data the API doesn't provide, build the honest fallback the note describes (or hide the part), never invented numbers. List every gap in your report. Don't add backend endpoints.


## Shell

- `shell/error` Something didn't load (area-shell.html): **A. Fail in place**. Recommended. Only the part that failed shows an error, sized like the content it replaces, with a retry and a reference for support. The rest of the page keeps working, which matters when the failure is one slow service.
- `shell/loading` Loading (area-shell.html): **A. Skeleton of the real layout**. Recommended. The frame loads instantly and the page shows its real structure in soft placeholders, with labels already in place. Nothing jumps when data lands, and it never pretends to have numbers it doesn't.
- `shell/empty` Empty, first run (area-shell.html): **B. The path to a first session**. Recommended. The four real steps, with the done one checked and the current one open: connect Alpaca, build, backtest, run on paper. It shows the whole route, including that paper comes before live, and the panel beside it previews what Home will look like.
- `shell/frame2` App frame, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Seven areas, clock in the bar**. Recommended. Markets becomes the seventh area, second after Home, because prices are something people check most days. The market clock moves into the bar beside search, so there's no tape and every page starts 34px higher. Seven names fit in the capsule at 1440 and still fit at 1280; narrower than that, the capsule has to drop to icons or move into a menu. The phone gets a Markets tab in the same position.
- `shell/phone-nav2` Phone navigation, round two (area-shell-2.html): **D. Built for checking in**. Recommended. The phone is treated as the place to check in during the day, and building stays on desktop: tabs for Home, Sessions, Markets, Activity and Account. Activity gets a tab instead of a bell because it's the main reason to open the app while the market is open. Strategies and backtests open read-only from a session or from search. Anyone who wants to build on the phone loses that path, so the read-only screens need a plain line saying editing is on the web.
- `shell/mode2` Paper and live, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Scope beside the page title**. Recommended. The scope moves into the page head, next to what it filters: All accounts, Live ••4821 or Paper ••PA92, each with its balance. Each page remembers its own choice, so Sessions can stay on paper while Home shows everything, and the choice is kept in the page's address so a shared link opens the same view. Per-page memory can surprise someone who switched on one page and expected it everywhere, which is why the control always shows its current value.
- `shell/palette2` Command palette, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Useful before you type**. Recommended. The palette opens on what's next today, your sessions with their state and today's move, and what you opened recently, so most trips take one arrow key and Enter. Typing narrows the same list, and Tab hands the query to Copilot. On Home the figures are already loaded; on other pages they cost one small request when the palette opens.
- `shell/notifications2` Notifications, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Today, drawn as lanes**. Recommended. The bell opens on today drawn the way Home draws time: one short lane per session from the open to the close, with each fill and the freeze where they happened and the 15:50 rebalance still ahead. Below it, the list starts with what needs you. It reads at a glance and matches the Home curve, so nothing new has to be learned. It's taller than a plain list, and on busy days the dots merge into counts.
- `shell/account2` Account menu, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Connections first**. Recommended. The menu leads with the two Alpaca accounts, since a broken connection is the one account problem that stops trading: each shows whether it's connected, when it was last checked and what runs on it. The plan, settings and sign-out follow. It answers the question people most often open it with. It needs a last-checked time per connection, which means recording each successful Alpaca call per account.
- `shell/attention2` Frozen sleeve on other pages, round two (area-shell-2.html): **A. Amber on Sessions**. Recommended. The amber mark sits on Sessions in the bar, the area where the frozen session lives, as well as on its lane on Home, so from any page you can see where to go and the bell stays for news. Sleeves only live under Sessions, so the mark always points to one place. It's small, which is fine because Home's amber line carries the first sighting.
- `shell/offline2` Lost connection, round two (area-shell-2.html): **B. Trading continues, said plainly**. Recommended. A neutral strip says what people most want to know when their connection drops: the sessions run on LlamaTrade's servers and keep trading. It gives the time of the figures on screen and retries on its own, and the clock and Now marker stop as in A. It stays grey because nothing is wrong with the account. The wording changes when the problem is Alpaca, as in C.

## Home

- `home/dashboard` Home on an ordinary day (area-home.html): **A. Account first**. Recommended. The account figures and one large equity chart share a rim-lit panel, then sessions and activity sit side by side. It keeps the structure Home has today, so the work is in spacing and hierarchy, and it holds up with one session or twenty.
- `home/first-session` The first hours of a first session (area-home.html): **B. Session spotlight**. Recommended. While there is one session, Home is about that session: what it holds against its targets, the first fills and what happens next. It answers the questions a first-time user has today, and gives way to the normal Home once there is history to show.
- `home/markets` Markets (area-home.html): **B. A Markets page**. Recommended. Markets gets its own page: a watchlist that starts with what your sleeves hold, a large price chart and, under it, which sleeves hold the symbol and how much. Home's chart stays about your money, and a price is always one click from the positions it affects.
- `home/states` Loading and service errors (area-home.html): **B. Keep what loaded**. Recommended. Each panel loads and fails on its own. A figure that couldn't refresh keeps its last value with the time it was taken, and only the panel whose service failed shows an error with Try again. Nothing that is known gets hidden.
- `home/layout2` Home on an ordinary day, round two (area-home-2.html): **A. The curve, annotated**. Recommended. One large equity curve with a lane per session under it, so rebalances, starts, the stop on Nov 12 and this morning's fills sit on the same time axis as the money they moved. The lanes run past Now into the next two weeks, which puts what already happened and what is scheduled in one place. It costs some vertical room per session, so past about eight sessions the lanes would need to fold into one combined row.
- `home/hero` The top of Home (area-home-2.html): **B. Paper and live, split**. Recommended. The total stays, but directly under it a bar and two figures show how much is real money and how much is simulated, and the chart can show either. Today 62.5% of the headline is paper, so a single total on its own overstates what is at risk. It costs one row and a toggle, and it still works when everything is paper or everything is live, since the bar becomes one colour.
- `home/sessions-block` Sessions on Home (area-home-2.html): **D. Lanes by account**. Recommended. Sessions sit in two lanes, live above paper, and each card's width follows its share of the lane, so size and kind of money read before any number. It pairs with the paper and live split in the hero and keeps a live order from ever looking like a simulated one. Costs: one live session gets a lane to itself, and very small sessions get narrow cards that need a minimum width.
- `home/activity` What happened and what's next (area-home-2.html): **B. One timeline, past and next**. Recommended. Fills and scheduled rebalances share one timeline with a Now line between them, and the next two weeks of schedule run on below. Good at answering both what happened and what happens next in one read, and every row comes from data the app already stores: fills and each session's schedule. Costs: the planned rows can say when, not which orders, so they stay plain until there is a way to preview a rebalance.
- `home/attention2` When something needs you, round two (area-home-2.html): **B. The session, opened beside the account**. Recommended. The account keeps the left side of Home and the frozen session opens on the right with the share counts and three steps to resolve it, so you can fix it without leaving. The rejected order stays a highlighted row in the session list with its amount against the limit. It costs the chart half its width until the issue is cleared, which is the right trade on a morning like this.
- `home/first-visit2` First visit, round two (area-home-2.html): **B. Three ways to start** (user pick). Three large illustrated cards, one per way of making a strategy: from a template, by describing it to Copilot, or from blocks. Each card shows what that path looks like before you choose it. Clear and even-handed, but it adds a decision before anything happens, and someone unsure which to pick has no default.
- `home/quiet` After close, weekends and holidays (area-home-2.html): **A. Recap and what's next**. Recommended. Home keeps its usual layout and swaps the live parts for closed ones: the chart shows the last session from open to close, a panel shows what each session added to the day, and a short list says when the market opens and what runs then, including holidays and anything waiting on you. It works the same after close, on a weekend or before a holiday, and nothing moves when the market opens again.
- `home/phone` Home on the phone (area-home-2.html): **B. List first**. Recommended. The total and today's change take the top, then every session is one row with its sparkline, value and today's move, so all four fit on the first screen and twenty would scroll like any list. On scroll the total shrinks into the header and stays visible. It follows the same order as the desktop Home, so nothing has to be relearned, at the cost of a less striking first screen.

## Strategies

- `strategies/template-preview` Template preview (area-strategies.html): **A. Side drawer**. Recommended. The preview slides in over the gallery, so you keep your place and can step to the next template with the arrow keys. It holds the description, holdings, rules and backtest, with the decision at the bottom.
- `strategies/detail-overview` Strategy overview (area-strategies.html): **B. Plain story**. Recommended. One calm column that reads top to bottom: what the strategy does in a sentence, what it is doing today, how the paper session is going and how the backtest went. Each block links to its full view. Closest to the marketing site's tone, and the honest order: rules, then today, then results.
- `strategies/detail-holdings` Would hold today (area-strategies.html): **A. Decision path**. Recommended. Walks the rules with today's values filled in: the RSI check, the branch it picks and the branch it skips, then compares the result with the session. It answers 'why does it hold this?' without reading code.
- `strategies/detail-rules` Rules (area-strategies.html): **A. Numbered outline**. Recommended. The rules as numbered sentences with each value as a token, the same outline the editor and the version diff use, and a side panel with what the rules need: symbols, history and rebalance dates. Code is a toggle away.
- `strategies/detail-versions` Versions (area-strategies.html): **A. Timeline and diff**. Recommended. A version timeline on the left and, for the selected version, the changed lines in the outline plus the backtest before and after. It answers 'what did I change and did it help?' in one place, and says plainly which version the session runs.
- `strategies/detail-performance` Performance (area-strategies.html): **B. Paper against backtest**. Recommended. Compares the paper session with a backtest over exactly the same dates, explains the gap in plain terms (fill timing, fees, the cash kept back) and keeps the full backtest below. It answers the question that matters before going live: is it behaving as tested?
- `strategies/list2` Strategies list, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Stage track**. Recommended. Each strategy is a row on a track from draft to backtested to paper to live, drawn like the lanes on Home's annotated curve. The stage columns double as data columns, so a backtest return can only appear under Backtested and money only under Paper or Live; they can't be mistaken for each other. The dashed segment shows the next step, with its button at the end of the row. It costs width, so on a phone the track shrinks to four dots, and a strategy that went back from live to paper needs its history read from the dimmed cells.
- `strategies/empty2` No strategies yet, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Three ways over an empty track**. Recommended. The same three ways as Home's first visit, in smaller cards with the same pictures, so a new user meets one choice in two places. Under them the stage track from the list sits empty, with its four column headings, and says where the first strategy will appear and how it moves. It teaches the list's shape before there's anything in it, and it costs the cards some height compared with Home.
- `strategies/templates2` Templates gallery, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Start from a goal**. Recommended. The gallery opens on one question, what this money should do, with five plain goals. Choosing one narrows the 80 templates to the three that fit it best, side by side on the same ten-year backtest with SPY. It's the shortest path for someone who doesn't know the categories yet, and it says up front that falling less has usually meant growing less. Costs: every template needs its goals assigned by hand and kept current, and people who already know what they want need the Browse all 80 link to reach the full gallery.
- `strategies/preview2` Template preview, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. What it held when**. Recommended. The backtest curve sits over a band showing what the template held each month, on the same time axis, so each switch lines up with the move around it. Hovering any month names the holdings and the reason for the switch. It explains a rules-based template better than a list of rules, and for a fixed mix the band simply shows the weights staying put. Costs: backtest results today carry the curve, trades and final positions, so the monthly holdings have to be rebuilt from trades or stored by the engine, and templates with many symbols need them grouped to stay readable.
- `strategies/overview2` Strategy overview, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Its own curve, annotated**. Recommended. Home's annotated curve, narrowed to one strategy: the paper sleeve since Jun 2 against a backtest of the same version over the same dates, with lanes for the branch it held, each rebalance and its fills, the version the session runs and the backtest's own choices. The lanes continue past Now to Dec 1, so the Thanksgiving closure and the next rebalance sit on the same axis. The second frame shows the same page for a strategy with no session: the curve becomes the backtest and the future column offers a start instead of a schedule. It needs the backtest re-run over the session's dates, which is one extra backtest per session.
- `strategies/holdings2` Would hold today, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Said in a sentence**. Recommended. Two short paragraphs answer the question with today's values set inline: what it would hold, the reading that decided it, and how the session compares. A small scale shows both lines, 65 for the session's version and 70 for the saved one, and a compact table carries the exact weights. It explains why in words a new user can follow and still gives the numbers. The sentences come from fixed templates per rule shape, so a strategy with deeply nested conditions would fall back to round one's decision path.
- `strategies/rules2` Rules, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **C. Rules on the timeline**. Recommended. The longest backtest's curve with a band under it showing which branch held each month, which pair momentum picked and SPY's RSI against the line at 70. Selecting a month explains that rebalance in a sentence. It's Home's annotated curve applied to one strategy, so the rules read as behaviour over time: here, 20 of 94 months in treasuries and gold. The cost is that the band needs one colour per branch, so strategies with more than about five leaves would group the rest into one shade, and it needs the backtest to store each rebalance's branch.
- `strategies/versions2` Versions, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. Versions on the curve**. Recommended. The strategy's whole life on one time axis: the paper session's curve, a lane showing which version was latest, a lane for the session with its rebalances, and a lane of backtest runs. The gap between saving version 3 and the session still running version 2 is visible as two lanes that disagree, with the restart action beside it. It matches Home's annotated curve. It needs a session or backtest to have something to draw, so a strategy with only saved versions shows the lanes and no curve.
- `strategies/performance2` Performance, round two (area-strategies-2.html): **A. The gap, annotated**. Recommended. The difference between paper and the same-dates backtest drawn as its own line, so each step lines up with the rebalance that caused it, and each step is numbered and explained beside the chart. It answers whether the strategy behaves as tested and why it doesn't quite, with the leftover shown so an unexplained gap is visible too. Attributing a step to fill timing needs the open and close prices for each fill day, which the app already has from market data; it can't explain a gap caused by something outside the strategy, such as an order placed in Alpaca directly.

## Builder

- `builder/start2` Starting a draft (area-builder-2.html): **A. The draft opens at once**. Recommended. New strategy opens the editor straight away with an untitled draft, and the empty rules area holds the three ways to start side by side: describe it, copy a template, or add the first block. Nobody has to choose before they see where they'll work, and every path ends in the same editor. One gap: the API creates a strategy only with code, so an untitled draft lives in this browser until it's saved as version 1, and it needs a local draft store and a clear line saying so.
- `builder/blocks2` Blocks view, round two (area-builder-2.html): **A. Rules over their history**. Recommended. The rules stay as sentences, and under them each side of the condition gets a lane on the same dates as the last backtest, so you can see that the defensive side held at 8 of 24 monthly rebalances and where. Selecting a rule lights up its lane. It uses Home's annotated-curve language, which makes the editor and Home read the same way. The cost is data: today a backtest stores fills, not which branch each rebalance chose, so the lanes need the backtest to record that decision. They also show version 3 until version 4 is saved and run.
- `builder/condition2` Editing a condition (area-builder-2.html): **A. The threshold on its own history**. Recommended. Clicking 72 opens the condition under its rule with SPY's 14-day RSI drawn over the last two years, the new threshold as a line you can drag and version 3's 70 as a dashed one. The shaded stretches and the counts underneath say how often the condition held: 36 days and 6 of 24 monthly rebalances at 72, against 50 days and 8 at 70. It turns a guess into a choice. The cost is data: the engine only computes RSI inside a backtest, so this needs daily bars for the signal symbol and an indicator calculation the editor can call, which the API doesn't offer yet.
- `builder/weights2` Editing weights (area-builder-2.html): **A. One bar to drag, numbers to type**. Recommended. The When side's weights are one bar: drag the edge between TLT and GLD, or type in the rows below, and the other weight moves so the side stays at 100%. The seven methods the engine parses sit above it, with market-cap shown but unavailable and the reason given. The rail turns the split into dollars on the paper session's current equity, which makes 60/40 concrete. It fits strategies with up to about six symbols per block; beyond that the bar's segments get too thin to grab and the rows do the work.
- `builder/draft2` Copilot drafts a strategy (area-builder-2.html): **B. Questions first, then the draft**. Before writing anything, Copilot asks about the parts of the description that could mean several things: which ETFs, what counts as overbought, how to split bonds and gold. Each question is a row of choices with a sensible one picked, and the draft fills in on the right as they're answered. The first draft needs fewer corrections, because the guesses become choices you saw. It's slower for someone who'd rather fix a draft than answer questions, so Skip writes the draft with the defaults at any point.
- `builder/code2` Code view, round two (area-builder-2.html): **A. Completions that know the form**. Recommended. A breadcrumb above the code shows where the cursor sits in the tree, and completions offer only what that form accepts: inside weight that's :lookback, :method and :top, and after :method the seven methods, with market-cap shown but disabled because the validator rejects it. Every suggestion comes from the vocabulary the parser already pins, so nothing here needs new API. It helps most while learning the language and costs little once you know it, though the completion list has to stay in step with the parser on every release.
- `builder/split2` Split view, round two (area-builder-2.html): **A. Aligned rows**. Recommended. Each rule in words sits on the same row as the lines of code it becomes, like a text printed beside its translation. There's nothing to match up by eye: the selected row is one band across both halves, and an edit on either side updates its neighbour in place. It reads best for strategies up to a screen or two. Rows take the height of whichever side is taller, so long asset lists leave gaps on the words side, and deep nesting needs indentation on both sides to stay readable.
- `builder/problems2` Problems and checks, round two (area-builder-2.html): **A. Errors block, warnings ask**. Recommended. The save button carries the state: with an error it's disabled and says what to fix, and with only warnings it reads Save with 2 warnings and opens a short confirmation that lists them in the validator's own words. It follows the rule the validator already encodes, so a strategy that can't run is never saved and one with relative weights still can be. It costs one extra click on every save with warnings. One gap: client-side warnings carry a block and field rather than a line, so pointing at line 7 needs the parser's source positions mapped onto blocks.
- `builder/copilot2` Copilot edits in the editor, round two (area-builder-2.html): **A. The proposal on the history lanes**. Recommended. The change is shown on a time axis from Nov 2024 to Nov 2026: both backtest curves on top, and under them a lane for each version showing the months it held bonds and gold. The proposal adds three defensive months (9 of 24 against 6), and you can see they sit around the spring drawdown, which is why the drawdown falls from −13.4% to −9.1%. It uses the same visual language as Home, and the exact code change sits beside it. The gap: Copilot needs to backtest unsaved code to produce this, and backtests today run only on saved versions, so it needs a draft-backtest call or a hidden saved version.

## Backtests

- `backtests/setup2` Backtest setup, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Pick the range on the data**. Recommended. The date range is chosen on a strip of SPY's price from Jan 2016, the first date LlamaTrade has bars for, with a lane per symbol the strategy reads underneath. The warm-up the indicators need before the start is shaded, so a range that can't work is visible before you run it. Settings sit in a column beside it. The cost is that the backend has no call that returns each symbol's first bar date, so the lanes need one added to the market data service.
- `backtests/missing-history` Not enough history for a symbol (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Caught at setup, with the fixes priced**. Recommended. Setup shows DBMF's lane hatched where it has no bars and puts the earliest workable start, after DBMF's warm-up, on the same axis. Two fixes sit beside it, each with what it costs in years and rebalances, and the Run button waits until one is chosen. Nothing fails after the fact. It needs the market data service to return each symbol's first bar date, which it doesn't today.
- `backtests/running2` Run in progress, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. The curve, drawing in**. Recommended. The run page is the annotated curve from results, drawn up to the simulated date: the curve, each switch between the defensive and growth sides, every symbol's holding lane and the monthly rebalances appear as the run reaches them, and the unrun years stay empty. The phases sit above it so 57% reads as progress through the work, not through the dates. It needs the progress stream to carry equity points and fills; today it sends status, phase message, current date and partial metrics only, so the side figures work now and the curve and lanes would need either new stream fields or polling the run.
- `backtests/failed` A run that fails, or is cancelled (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Keep what ran**. Recommended. The failed run keeps its annotated curve up to the day it stopped, with an amber line at Aug 9, 2024, the missing XLU days hatched on its lane and the plain explanation beside it. You can see the run was doing well and exactly where the data let it down, and the fixes are concrete. Two gaps: the service doesn't keep equity points for failed runs today, and runs can't resume, so every fix starts again from Jan 2021. The explanation is written from status_message, which is a free string, so it needs a structured error code to be reliable.
- `backtests/long-range` A very long range (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Log scale, hard stretches shaded**. Recommended. Over ten years the curve switches to a log scale so a 10% fall in 2018 and in 2024 look the same size, and three hard stretches (the 2018 fourth-quarter fall, March 2020 and the 2022 rate rises) are shaded and compared in the side column. The defensive lane is compressed into one row so 130 rebalances don't crowd the page. It answers the first question about a long test, how it held up when markets fell, but the stretches are a list we would maintain by hand; the API has no notion of them.
- `backtests/results2` Results, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. The run, annotated**. Recommended. One plain sentence and six figures, then the curve against SPY with everything the strategy did drawn on the same dates: the six moves into TLT and GLD shaded, the worst drawdown marked, a lane per symbol showing when it was held and a tick for every monthly rebalance. It is the home curve's language applied to a backtest, so the reason for each bend is on screen. The API has no rebalance log or weights over time, so the lanes are rebuilt from the trade list's entry and exit dates, and a switch is a rebalance where the held symbols changed.
- `backtests/months-trades2` Months and trades, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. One year, every rebalance**. Recommended. The results curve zoomed to a single year, with the same lanes, and the twelve rebalances laid out under their dates with the orders each one placed. Selecting one opens the trades and the reasons the engine recorded. It keeps the annotated language from the results page and connects each month's return to what was bought and sold. Reasons come from each trade's entry and exit reason text; there is no rebalance log, so a rebalance's orders are grouped by fill date.
- `backtests/history2` Backtest history, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Runs on a strategy timeline**. Recommended. One lane per strategy across the calendar, with a marker for every run on the day you made it, its result beside it and a bar for the session it became. It shows in one place how each idea developed and when a newer version beats what is already trading, as with All-Weather v3 against the live v1. Runs don't record which session they started, so the session bars need that link stored; past about eight strategies the lanes would need a filter.
- `backtests/compare2` Compare runs, round two (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Annotated overlay**. Recommended. The curves share one scale, and under them each run gets a lane of what it held, with the stretches where the two held different symbols outlined on both the lanes and the chart. The difference line at the bottom then shows what each of those stretches was worth. It explains the gap rather than only measuring it, and works best for two or three runs. CompareBacktests returns metrics only, so the page fetches each run's curve and trades separately and derives holdings from the trades.
- `backtests/phone` Backtests on the phone (area-backtests-2.html): **A. Latest result on top**. Recommended. The tab opens on a list, but the newest finished run sits at the top as a card with its curve and three figures, and a run in progress sits above it with its progress. It covers the common visit (did my run finish, and how did it do) without hiding the history, and it reads the same on desktop. Setting up a new run stays possible on the phone but isn't the focus.

## Sessions

- `sessions/pause-stop` Pause and stop (area-sessions.html): **A. Plain dialog**. Recommended. Stop asks once, in a short dialog that names what stays, what ends and that it can't be undone, and offers pause as the gentler choice. On the phone both live in one action sheet, and a paused session shows a clear banner with Resume.
- `sessions/list2` Sessions list, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. Every session on one timeline**. Recommended. Home's annotated curve, turned into the list: each session is a row with its own small curve since it started, its rebalances, starts, stop and today's fills marked on one shared date axis, and its next run in the shaded part after Now. Live and paper keep separate headings and totals. You can see at a glance which sessions are busy, which are new and what runs before the close. It costs about 60px per session, so past twelve or so the rows need a compact mode without the curves.
- `sessions/detail2` A running session, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. Its own annotated curve**. Recommended. The session gets the same picture as Home, scoped to one sleeve: its equity since Jun 2 with the mix changes pinned, one lane per symbol showing every buy and sell on its date, and a check lane with a tick for each scheduled check. The lanes run past Now into the next two weeks, so 15:50 today and the Thanksgiving closure sit on the same axis as the history. Holdings against target and today's fills sit underneath. It costs height per symbol, so a sleeve with more than about six symbols needs its lanes folded into one.
- `sessions/rebalance2` One rebalance, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. The mix over time, one check opened**. Recommended. The sleeve's mix since Jun 2 is drawn as stacked bands, with the target as a dashed line and a lane of checks underneath on the same axis, in the annotated-timeline language from Home. Today's check is selected and opened on the right: the inputs, the branch of the rule that ran, before and after for each symbol and the two orders. Past the Now line the bands continue as the planned 60 / 40. You see this rebalance in the context of all the others, and stepping to another is one click on the lane. Weight history needs a daily holdings series per sleeve; the ledger can rebuild it from fills and prices, but nothing stores it yet.
- `sessions/order2` A rejected order, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. The order against its limit**. Recommended. The page leads with the one thing that went wrong: a bar for the order's value with the $10,000 limit drawn across it and the $1,210.84 over it in amber. Below it every check is listed with what it found, and the three ways forward sit on the right with what each one does. The bar uses the order's estimated value and the session's limit, which are both stored. Showing the other checks as clear is inferred from the stored list of violations, which only holds text for checks that failed, so a check that was skipped (position size, daily loss and order rate need database access) would read the same as one that passed.
- `sessions/frozen2` A frozen sleeve, and resolving it, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. The lane turns amber, resolved beside it**. Recommended. The session keeps its annotated lanes, and the session and SMH lanes turn amber from 09:31, as decided for Home, with a tick for every five-minute check that still found the difference. The three steps to resolve it sit on the right, so you see the freeze in time and fix it on one screen; the second phone shows the lane ending when the sleeve resumes. Data gaps: there is no endpoint today to record a resolution or resume a sleeve (DriftCorrected and SleeveResumed are catalogued but never emitted), so both steps need building, and the ticks need each reconciliation run to be stored.
- `sessions/deploy2` Deploy to paper, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. Placed on the timeline**. Recommended. The paper account's sessions are drawn as lanes on one time axis, and the new session's lane appears among them with its start today, its first rebalance on Dec 1 and the one after on Jan 4. It answers the question people tend to have after pressing start, which is when anything will happen, and it uses the same picture as Home. Every date comes from the strategy's schedule and the market calendar, so nothing here needs new data. It takes more room than a plain form, and with many sessions the lanes would fold the same way Home's do.
- `sessions/golive2` From paper to live (area-sessions-2.html): **A. Paper record, then one confirmation**. Recommended. The left side shows what you're basing the decision on: the paper session's curve since Jun 2 with its rebalances marked, and a live lane starting at Now with its next four rebalances. The right side is a short review with one plain statement about real money, one checkbox and a button that names the amount. Nothing is amber, because nothing is wrong. It is honest about the API: there is no promote call, so this starts a new live session with its own sleeve, and the paper session's history stays where it is. The paper session keeps running by default, which costs nothing and keeps a comparison going.
- `sessions/stop2` Stopping a session, round two (area-sessions-2.html): **A. Where everything goes**. Recommended. The dialog draws what happens to each holding: TLT and GLD move to Unmanaged, the cash moves to Unallocated, and the 15:50 rebalance is crossed off on a short lane that ends in a stop marker. It describes exactly what the API does today, so nothing in it is a promise we'd have to build. Pause is offered once, with what it keeps. For the live All-Weather Portfolio the same dialog adds one line that the holdings stay in the live account as real positions. It's the tallest of the dialogs.
- `sessions/phone2` Sessions on the phone (area-sessions-2.html): **B. Needs you, then the list**. Recommended. Anything that needs a decision sits in one amber card at the top, one row per issue with the number that matters: here the frozen Momentum Sectors (Alpaca 43 SMH, ledger 31) and Recession Radar's rejected buy of 124 TLT over the $10,000 limit. Below it the sessions split into running and stopped, with value and today's move. It follows Home's phone decision (list first, amber only where something waits on you), so the tab and Home feel like one app, and on a quiet day the amber card simply isn't there. It shows no timing beyond the next rebalance; that lives one tap down.

## Portfolio

- `portfolio/frozen` Frozen sleeve (area-portfolio.html): **A. Difference first**. Recommended. One amber statement of the problem, then a symbol table where the single differing row is the only coloured thing on the page, with the steps and held orders beside it. It's honest about what's known and what isn't automated, and it works the same whether one symbol differs or three.
- `portfolio/allocate` Top up a sleeve (area-portfolio.html): **A. Top-up dialog**. Recommended. Pick the sleeve, set the amount with a field or a slider capped at what's unallocated, and read the move as two from-to lines. It says plainly when the cash gets invested, which is the question people have after a top-up, and frozen sleeves simply aren't offered.
- `portfolio/first-run` Before the first sleeve (area-portfolio.html): **B. Three steps**. Recommended. A short checklist where two steps are already done (account connected, funds available) and the third offers three templates with their backtest figures to deploy from. It shows progress, makes the next action obvious and gives the empty page real content.
- `portfolio/overview2` Portfolio overview, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. Money in, annotated**. Recommended. Home's annotated curve, turned toward money rather than trades: total equity drawn over a dashed step line of money added, so the gap between them is what the sleeves earned, and one lane per sleeve marks each deposit, allocation and stop on the same dates. The statement underneath adds up to the total with Added, Value and Gain side by side. It's the clearest answer to how the account got this big, but it uses most of the screen for history, and the lanes need folding past about eight sleeves.
- `portfolio/accounts2` Paper and live, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **C. A split bar that filters**. Recommended. Under the total, the two Alpaca accounts are drawn as one bar split by size, live solid in ink and paper dashed, each half carrying its value, today's change and its sleeves. Selecting a half filters the statement to that account and dims the other half without hiding it, so the split is the navigation and the headline total stays, as Home decided. It works with any number of sleeves per account, but a live account much smaller than paper makes its half narrow, so the half needs a minimum width.
- `portfolio/sleeve2` One sleeve, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. A lane per holding**. Recommended. The annotated curve applied to one sleeve: its value against SPY, then a lane per holding with every buy and sell on its date, and a cash lane for money in and dividends. Past Now, the lanes show the 15:50 rebalance with the orders estimated from current prices. Holdings with cost, unrealized and weight against target sit below with today's fills. It explains why a weight is where it is without opening the activity log, but a sleeve with eight or more holdings needs its lanes folded.
- `portfolio/reconciliation2` Checking against Alpaca, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. A bridge from the ledger to Alpaca**. Recommended. Each account gets one verdict: live is a single quiet line, paper shows a bridge from the ledger's sleeves to the equity Alpaca reports, so the 12 SMH appear as dollars added and cash spent rather than only a count. The symbol table, the order that caused it and the three ways out sit beside it, with the two that need ledger changes marked as not built. It depends on reading Alpaca's order history to name the outside order, which the app doesn't do today.
- `portfolio/activity2` Activity, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. Lanes above the statement**. Recommended. The month's lanes sit above the statement, with fills counted on each sleeve's lane and money in and out marked on the same dates, and a range you drag across them filters the table. You find the week something happened by eye, then read its entries with each sleeve's running cash. It keeps the statement format that exports cleanly. The lanes get busy for sleeves that rebalance daily, so fills on the same day are merged into one counted marker.
- `portfolio/add-funds2` Adding money, round two (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. Add, then place**. Recommended. Adding paper money and placing it become one short flow: the amount, then where it goes, then a confirm. Unallocated stays the plain default, a sleeve top-up says when the cash will be invested, stopped sleeves are listed but can't be chosen, and a now-and-after bar shows the paper account changing. It saves the second trip people make right after adding funds, at the cost of one more step for those who only wanted to add them.
- `portfolio/markets2` Markets (area-portfolio-2.html): **A. Watchlist, chart and holders**. Recommended. A watchlist that starts with every symbol your sleeves hold, each row showing which sleeves and how many shares, then the symbols you watch. The selected symbol gets a large daily chart with your own fills and each sleeve's average cost drawn on it, and a right column with every sleeve that holds it, live and paper kept apart, and its next rebalance. Prices come from Alpaca's IEX feed, which covers only part of market volume, and that's said under the chart.

## Copilot

- `copilot/empty` Copilot, empty (area-copilot.html): **A. Open canvas**. Recommended. The marketing hero carried into the app: bright art, the mark, one line on what Copilot does and a large composer with grouped starters. It is the calmest way in, and history stays one click away rather than taking a rail on every visit.
- `copilot/conversation` Conversation with tool calls (area-copilot.html): **B. Sources beside**. Recommended. Every claim carries a numbered citation, and the pane on the right shows what each tool call returned: the rule, the sleeve against SPY, the RSI days. It costs width, but for questions about your money the evidence should be visible, not folded away.
- `copilot/approval` Approve a run (area-copilot.html): **B. In the composer**. Recommended. While Copilot waits, the composer becomes the approval: the arguments are editable fields, Enter approves and Escape declines. You can fix a date without another message, the thread stays readable, and the answer is written back into the thread as a one-line record.
- `copilot/page2` The Copilot page, round two (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Choose what to ask about**. Recommended. A list of your account, sessions, strategies and recent backtests sits on the left, and picking one shows exactly which records Copilot will read for it, questions written for its current state and a composer scoped to it. You know what an answer is based on before you ask, and the starters are always relevant. It costs a column on every visit, and a question that spans two sessions needs the Account subject or a second chip.
- `copilot/explain2` Explaining a session, with sources (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Citations on the curve**. Recommended. The answer sits on the left and the session's own curve sits beside it in Home's annotated style, with each citation number placed on the date it refers to: the RSI close on Nov 4, the switch on Nov 5, this morning's fills, and the 15:50 check past the Now line. Reading the answer and checking it against the chart happen in one glance. It needs a fills tool the agent doesn't have yet (today it can read positions, performance and the strategy, not individual fills), and the chart only works for answers about one session over a stretch of dates.
- `copilot/draft2` From a sentence to the builder (area-copilot-2.html): **C. Lands in the builder, choices marked**. Recommended. The draft opens straight in the builder as an unsaved draft, with Copilot docked on the left so you can keep asking for changes. Blocks that come from your sentence are solid; values Copilot chose are dashed and tagged, and selecting one explains the choice and offers alternatives. You edit with the real tools from the first second and nothing has to be copied over. It depends on the builder accepting an unsaved draft from Copilot with per-block notes, which is new work in both places.
- `copilot/confirm2` Actions Copilot proposes (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Two kinds of action**. Recommended. Actions that don't touch orders (saving a version, running a backtest) are ticked in one card and confirmed together. The session switch sits in a separate dashed card that says plainly Copilot can't do it and opens the session's own change screen with version 5 filled in. The line between proposing and trading is visible in the layout itself. Two cards for one request is more to read, and the handoff screen needs a way to accept prefilled values from Copilot. The strategy language has no condition for RSI above 70 on each of the last three closes, so version 5 assumes one is added.
- `copilot/panel2` Copilot on other pages (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Answer in place**. Recommended. An ask field sits in the page header. The answer opens as a block inside the page, directly under the panels it cites, and those panels are outlined while it's open. Nothing covers the page, the figures stay where you expect them, and Continue in Copilot carries the thread to the full page. It suits one or two questions; a longer back-and-forth pushes the page down, which is the point at which the full page is the better place.
- `copilot/history2` Finding an earlier answer (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Search finds the paragraph**. Recommended. Search runs across what was said, not only titles, and each result shows the matching paragraph with the term highlighted, what came out of the conversation and which session it was about. The selected result opens on the right at that paragraph. You find the answer itself in one step, which is what people come back for. It needs full-text search over messages, which the agent service doesn't offer yet (it lists sessions and messages but can't search them).
- `copilot/entry2` Ways in from the rest of the app (area-copilot-2.html): **A. Ask from a marker**. Recommended. Every marker on the annotated curve already opens a small card with what happened that day. The card gains two or three questions written for that event, and choosing one opens Copilot with the date, session and fills attached. The question starts from the thing you were looking at, so Copilot needs no guessing about what you mean. It only covers events that have a marker; a general question still needs the ⌘J shortcut or the Copilot page.
- `copilot/limits` Limits and outages (area-copilot-2.html): **B. Keep the question, show what still works**. Recommended. Your question is kept as an unsent draft with a clear line that nothing was sent, and beside it the page lists what answers the same need without the model: the session's rule with today's values, the fills, templates and the builder. Desktop shows the model unavailable; the phone shows the allowance used up. Someone who came with a question still leaves with most of the answer, from pages that read the records directly. The rule-with-values view is new work, and the app needs a health check on the model provider to say when the outage started.
- `copilot/phone` Copilot on a phone (area-copilot-2.html): **B. A half sheet over the screen you're on**. Recommended. From any screen, the Copilot button raises a half sheet that already knows the session you're looking at, so the weights and figures stay visible above it while you read the answer. Drag it up for a full conversation with sources. The Copilot tab stays for history. The same thread continues in the desktop side panel. It keeps the question next to its subject, which matters most on a small screen. The half sheet is short, so long answers start collapsed to three lines with Read more.
